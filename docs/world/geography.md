# Geography of Farstone

Farstone Island occupies ~98 km² in the Farr Archipelago, on the eastern coastal shelf.
Its extent is roughly **14 km north–south by 9 km east–west**, with a coastline of **62 km** that is "mostly rocky with three sand beaches and one tidal marsh."
A single mountain (Mt. Farstone, 620 m) dominates the center; a single river (the Farr) drains its southern flanks into a single harbor (Farston Harbor) on the south coast.
The island's chartered jurisdiction boundary is the high-tide line of that harbor.
Total island population is **~12,000** — about **10,400** in the town of Farston, the remaining **~1,600** scattered across the rural areas.

A weather-dependent ferry provides twice-daily service to the mainland from the Harbor District's eastern terminal; sailings are cancelled or rescheduled during severe storms.

## Regions and named places

### Farstone Island (the whole)

A ~98 km² granite island on the eastern coastal shelf: one mountain, one river, one harbor town, one tidal marsh, and a weather-held ferry to the mainland.
Single watershed (Mt. Farstone), single tidal regime, single wind exposure.
Jurisdiction boundary: the high-tide line of Farston Harbor.

### Mt. Farstone — the center

The central peak, **620 m**, dominates the island.
Granite outcrops above the treeline (bare rock increases above 500 m); mixed conifer-broadleaf forest below.
Its catchment is the watershed source for the *entire* island — streams from the **southern flanks** converge into the Farr River; streams from the **northern flanks** feed The Marsh.

The upper flanks are a **watershed-protection zone** by municipal water-supply ordinance: logging is restricted, and building permits within the zone route through Public Works for hydrologic review on top of standard Building & Safety review.
The summit and ridgeline are reached by a **marked trail** leaving from the top of Ridge Road; the trailhead is town-maintained, the trail informal beyond that point.

### The Farr River — the watercourse

The island's only meaningful watercourse.
Headwaters on Mt. Farstone's southern flanks; descends through the upper forest, past the **municipal well-field** (in the alluvial-aquifer recharge zone where watershed-protection ordinances apply), through **Farr Valley's** farmland, and into **Farston Harbor at the harbor's western edge**.

A **riparian-buffer ordinance** designates a 30-meter vegetated buffer along both banks from the well-field down to tidewater; most landowners observe it, but two Farr Valley dairies chronically ignore it.
On outgoing tides the river's freshwater plume extends into the harbor and past the breakwater; its extent is the boundary used for shellfish-closure decisions during heavy-rainfall events.

### The Town of Farston — the harbor side

The municipal town on the harbor side of the island, holding ~10,400 residents and serving as the seat of the city council and department directors.
Town Hall is a converted Victorian on Main Street.
Chartered jurisdiction extends to the high-tide line of Farston Harbor.
Public Works maintains 47 miles of road and a water treatment plant fed from the mountain's catchment.
The town comprises four named neighborhoods:

#### Downtown Farston

The central business district along Main Street; the island's highest-density residential area.
Holds city hall (converted Victorian), the records clerk's office, the volunteer fire station, post office, public library, general store, the Farston Island Clinic, and small businesses.
Residential blocks back the commercial strip on three sides.
Notable detail: Hydrant-23 stands at the corner of 5th and Walnut; the pothole at Elm Street and 3rd Avenue is citizen complaint #2026-117.

#### Harbor District

The working waterfront on the south side of town: ferry terminal (eastern end of the harbor), fish-pier, harbormaster's office, recreational moorings, three boatyards, a chandlery, and harbor-side cafés and outfitters.
The Farr River meets the sea at the harbor's western edge.
The ferry runs to the mainland twice daily, weather-dependent; severe-storm cells trigger temporary closures via the harbormaster's office.

#### The Ridge

The town's neighborhood on Mt. Farstone's **southwest shoulder** — residential lots stair-stepping up the slope from downtown's upper end to about **200 m above sea level**, beyond which the watershed-protection zone forbids new building.
About **800** residents in larger-than-downtown, partly-wooded lots.
The upper streets sit near the gravity-feed limit of the municipal water system; Public Works maintains a small **pump station at Ridge-and-Mountainview** to boost pressure for the upper blocks.
The Mt. Farstone trailhead is at the top of Ridge Road.

#### Marsh-End

The town's **northeast** neighborhood, where Main Street's grid loosens into rural lots before giving way to The Marsh.
Low-density single-family homes on quarter-acre to two-acre lots; older houses near the original road, post-war infill closer to the marsh.
About **600** residents.
The seaward edge at the Marsh boundary is contested in places — The Marsh expands and contracts with sea-level events, and a parcels-near-marsh map is updated after major coastal storms.
Marsh-End falls *barely* within the volunteer fire department's 8-minute response radius from downtown.

### Farston Harbor — the embayment

The south-coast embayment fronting the town, protected by a granite **breakwater** and deep enough at most tides for the ferry, fishing boats, and recreational moorings.
The Farr River meets it at the western edge; the ferry terminal sits at the eastern end.
The harbor's high-tide line is the chartered jurisdiction boundary of the town: above it, the council and departments hold authority; below it, harbor and channel matters route through the harbormaster's office and the mainland Coast Guard.
The aging breakwater's western face took storm damage in winter 2024 (an FY2027 budget request is pending).

### Farr Valley — the farmland

The agricultural river valley running from Mt. Farstone's southern flanks down to the upper edge of town. **~140 hectares** across **~22 farmsteads**: hay, vegetable row crops, two small orchards, four operating dairies.
Population **~350**.
Sits *outside* the town's chartered municipal boundary but inside the polity's substrate scope; land-use authority is incorporated through county-style records (forthcoming).
Public Works samples three valley well sites quarterly per Charter Article III §4.

### North Shore — the rural north coast

The rural strip along the island's north coast: scattered homes on long lots running back from the shore, separated by conifer-broadleaf forest and intermittent streams.
Population **~320**.
The part of the island **farthest from emergency response** — the fire department's mutual-aid agreement with the mainland county covers north-shore incidents when town units can't make the response window, and the mainland fireboat is the primary reach for marine emergencies.
There are **no public hydrants** on the north shore; firefighting water is drafted from streams or the sea — a known gap, periodically discussed at council and never funded.

### The Marsh — the northeast corner

The salt marsh occupying the island's northeast corner: **~3 km²** of Spartina-dominated tidal grass, channels, and brackish flats.
Bird-rich, holding a soft-shell **clam bed** and a state-listed **common-tern breeding colony**.
The clam bed closes to harvest during summer warm spells (water temperature + recent rainfall trigger the closure), which the records clerk's office publishes.
The boundary with Marsh-End is contested in places — sea-level events have moved the high-water line by tens of meters in some sections.
Managed under a state shoreline-protection designation; the city council's authority is limited to public access and no-build zones within its sliver of jurisdiction.

### South Point Light — the southern tip

The automated lighthouse at the island's southern tip, marking the approach to Farston Harbor and the Farr River bar. **Established 1872, automated since 1987**; structure and lamp maintained by the mainland Coast Guard.
The surrounding parcel is town-owned and leased to the **Farstone Historical Society** for a seasonal museum.
Referenced in emergency dispatch — an offshore-distress witness arriving at the harbormaster's office triggers consultation of the light's visibility log.

## How the places connect (list-form map)

The connections below are reconstructed from the place descriptions and the ASCII sketch (orientation north-up).
They describe regional adjacency, not engine-level room exits — for the playable harbor MUD's ~30 walkable rooms (tutorial cove, harbor quay, market row, old town, lighthouse interior, hinterland) see the MUD canon, where all exits are bidirectional.

- **Mt. Farstone** is the hub of the island's interior.
  - South flanks → headwater streams → **Farr River**.
  - North flanks → headwater streams → **The Marsh**.
  - Southwest shoulder → **The Ridge** (trailhead at top of Ridge Road).
  - Upper flanks are the watershed-protection zone (restricted).

- **The Farr River** runs the central axis, north-to-south: Mt. Farstone (south flanks) → upper forest → **municipal well-field** → **Farr Valley** → **Downtown Farston / Farston Harbor** (enters at the harbor's **western edge**).

- **Farr Valley** lies between Mt. Farstone's flanks and the upper edge of town; its lower end borders **Downtown Farston** (the river threads between them).

- **The Ridge** climbs from **Downtown Farston's** upper end up Mt. Farstone's southwest shoulder (sea level → ~200 m), ending at the **Mt. Farstone trailhead**.

- **Downtown Farston** sits at the river mouth on the south coast, at the **western** end of Farston Harbor.
  It connects:
  - up-slope (NW) → **The Ridge**
  - up-valley (W/NW) → **Farr Valley**
  - south → **Harbor District** (the waterfront)
  - northeast along Main Street → **Marsh-End**

- **Harbor District** runs along the south coast from Downtown's southern/eastern edge.
  The **ferry terminal** is at the harbor's **eastern** end; from there the ferry crosses to the **mainland**.

- **Marsh-End** stretches **northeast** from Downtown along Main Street, its seaward fringe abutting **The Marsh**.

- **The Marsh** occupies the **northeast corner**, bounded on its south by Marsh-End and fed from the north by Mt. Farstone's north-flank streams.

- **North Shore** stretches along the **entire north coast**, the rural strip farthest from the town's response infrastructure.

- **Farston Harbor** fronts the town on the **south coast**, behind the granite breakwater. **South Point Light** marks the island's **southern tip** at the harbor approach.

- **Ferry**: Harbor District (eastern terminal) ⇆ mainland, twice daily, weather-permitting.
