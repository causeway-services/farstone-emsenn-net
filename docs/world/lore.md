# The Lore of Farstone

This file is the durable record of Farstone's history, backstory, themes, and — most importantly to preserve across every future engine rewrite — its **tone and voice**.
Farstone is **data, not code**.
The mechanics will be rebuilt many times; what must survive intact is what kind of place this is and how it is meant to feel.

---

## What Farstone Is

Farstone is a small New England harbor island in a coastal archipelago, fundamentally grounded in the mundane realities of municipal governance and salt-worn maritime life.
Its identity is defined **not by fantasy or spectacle but by the intersection of tide, fog, weather-held ferry schedules, permit deadlines, and the contradictions that emerge when a small town tries to govern itself fairly.** The most interesting events here are water-quality disputes, permits aging past their decision windows, and the slow work of keeping infrastructure intact.

It is explicitly **not** a generic fantasy world.
It is an identifiable place: granite bedrock, mixed northern forest, semidiurnal Atlantic tides, a cool maritime climate band (150 frost-free days), real infrastructure (twice-daily ferry, water treatment plant, a city hall in a converted Victorian, a volunteer fire department, a lighthouse).
The calendar is real-world — fiscal years, council elections, growing-season windows, tide tables.
The money is real (a chandler's purse holds 1200p; a player starts with 250p).
The skills are craft skills — knife-work, smithing, net-mending, clam-digging.
The resources are real resources — smoked fish, ship's biscuit, shellfish beds, rope, iron, oak.

## Geographic & Physical Backstory

Farstone Island is roughly oval — ~14 km north-to-south by 9 km east-to-west, ~98 km², in the Farr Archipelago off a cool maritime coast.
Its coastline runs 62 km, mostly rocky, with three sand beaches and one tidal salt marsh.
Everything about the island flows from a single fact of geography: **one mountain, one watershed, one river, one harbor.** Mt Farstone (620 m granite peak) is the hydrologic and geographic heart — nearly every freshwater source traces to its catchment.
The Farr River descends its southern flanks, passes the municipal well-field, runs through Farr Valley's farmland, and empties into Farston Harbor.
North-flank streams drain instead to The Marsh.
This single-catchment shape is why watershed protection, riparian buffers, and shellfish-closure rules sit at the center of the island's politics — there is no second river to fall back on.

The town of Farston (~10,400 of the island's ~12,000 residents) wraps the south coast around Farston Harbor, sheltered by a granite breakwater.
Four named neighborhoods — Downtown, the Harbor District, The Ridge (climbing the mountain's southwest shoulder to the 200-m watershed-protection line), and Marsh-End (loosening into rural lots toward the marsh) — plus rural Farr Valley, the North Shore, and the edges of The Marsh make up the inhabited island.

## Historical Setting

Farstone's history is rooted in real maritime settlement patterns:

- **1872** — South Point Light established at the island's southern tip, marking the harbor approach and the Farr River bar.
- **1923 (April 12)** — The City of Farston Charter adopted, the founding civic document.
  (Last amended November 8, 2024.)
- **1987** — South Point Light automated; its structure passes to mainland Coast Guard maintenance, the surrounding town-owned parcel later leased to the Farstone Historical Society for a seasonal museum.
- **2018** — Environmental survey identifies the Mt Farstone watershed forest as *old-growth-with-disturbance*; logging restricted by ordinance.
- **2024** — A winter storm damages the western face of the aging breakwater; the FY2027 budget request to repair it is pending.

The town carries 100+ years of municipal records and ordinances.
The lighthouse, the charter, and the breakwater are the three deep anchors of its institutional memory.

## Governance & Civic Character

The City of Farston is governed by its charter, which establishes **five hard-core constitutive commitments** (amendable only by two-thirds referendum):

1. **Public Health & Safety** — water-quality standards to be met or exceeded.
2. **Fiscal Responsibility** — operating within an approved annual budget; the finance director's reconciliation is the substrate of record.
3. **Transparent Deliberation** — all council deliberations public; minutes published within 5 business days; citizens hold a petition right.
4. **Equal Treatment Under Local Law** — services without neighborhood preference, except in charter-defined districts.
5. **Jurisdictional Boundaries** — each office acts within its defined scope; *ultra vires* acts are voidable.

Article III binds the operating rhythm: permit decisions within 30 calendar days, quarterly water-inspection reporting, and a budget cycle (draft by April 15, final adoption by June 15).
Citizens are active deliberators — they file complaints, speak at council, articulate positions.
Transparency and participation are **structural, not aspirational.**

## The Off-Season: The Harbor's Story

Beneath the municipal frame is the harbor's lived story, and it is a story of **scarcity and the lean season.** The summer trade has gone, the fleet is half-tied-up, money is short, and tempers on the quay are shorter.
The fishery is failing — the catch has thinned and the island can no longer feed everyone from its own waters.

In the designed MUD world (**Farstone-Tale, "The Off-Season"**), three pressures sit on the world from tick zero:

- **Scarce food** — the failing fishery.
  The hunger resources (clam bed, gull-egg ledge, the smokehouse's last smoked fish, the farm's root-cellar) carry deliberately small amounts.
  Every tick decays each NPC's hunger; the hungriest go to the nearest food and deplete it.
  When a flat runs dry, the actors who lived off it go hungry, their mood drops, and competition becomes real.
- **A disputed permit** — the dredging permit (Application 23), held within its decision window by the clerk.
  Granting it lets Coilin dredge a deep-water channel; that same dredging silts Brannaugh's clam ground.
  Ruling against the standing `within-window` cell — or the window simply closing — drives the permit to **⊤**: a contested fact the world *holds* rather than adjudicates away.
- **A feud** — between Coilin Vey (hot-headed, ambitious) and Brannaugh Ross (cold, tenacious) over the scarce catch and dredging rights.

Mood, the commons, and the disputed permit are all the world's own machinery — the tick does the rest without scripting.
Nobody wrote the story of the flats running dry; the systems produced it.
Nobody scripted the feud sharpening under scarcity; the social system read it off opposed traits and shared contested ground.

## Religion & Local Cult

In the harbor reading, **Maraset** — "the grey-eyed lady of tide and harbor" — is the island's tutelary deity.
Father Coll keeps the Chapel of Maraset, the place to worship and pray; the chapel-quiet is a recreation resource.
Beyond the chapel's sanctioned faith, **Morrigan the marsh-witch** trades cures, charms, and the spells the chapel frowns on, from a hut past the salt marsh — the outside edge of the social and moral map.
(The deeper theology of Maraset is not recorded; see Gaps.)

## Themes

- **Contradiction is a value, not a fault.** Farstone's signature mechanic is the bilattice fold: when a fact is contested (a hydrant passed by one inspector and failed by another; a permit both `within-window` and `overdue`), the world does not resolve it to true or false — it settles to **⊤ (TOP)** and *holds* it.
  The contradiction becomes a situated value that actors carry as a contested-belief mood penalty.
  This is the thematic core: **a small town trying to govern itself fairly will produce contradictions, and an honest world shows them rather than hiding them.**
- **Scarcity makes stories.** Needs decay, resources deplete, the fishery fails.
  Drama is not authored; it emerges from a lean world pressing on its inhabitants.
- **The mundane is the drama.** Tides, fog, ferry cancellations, permit deadlines, water-quality thresholds — these *are* the events.
  There are no dragons; there is a snow-removal budget overrun.
- **Everyone sees a different world.** Each actor perceives through its own Umwelt (its `perceives` set).
  Two NPCs in the same room sense different things and so carry different moods.
  The same world state produces genuinely different stories per fiber.
- **Value is richness, not assignment.** A thing's worth emerges from the density and settledness of its accumulated cells; trade is the exchange of those cells; scarcity is settledness.
  The economy is a property of the substrate, not a number bolted on.

## Tone & Voice — to be preserved across every rewrite

This is the most load-bearing section of the file.
Whatever the engine, **keep this feel.**

- **Deliberately mundane.** Farstone is grounded, municipal, salt-worn.
  It is the "working waterfront of a granite island after the summer trade has gone."
  It is not epic; it is a real place with real, small problems.
  The reason it works as a testbed is precisely that its contradictions are *real* (a water-quality dispute, a permit aging past its window) and the world doesn't cheat by resolving them away.
- **Lean, competitive, pressed.** The off-season mood is the default key.
  Money is short, the catch is thin, tempers are short.
  Warmth exists (Nessa Cawl's hearth, the taproom, Sister Edda's care) but it is warmth *against* the cold, not abundance.
- **The world holds its contradictions.** Never write Farstone so that a dispute tidily resolves on its own.
  A disputed fact is a ⊤ cell the world carries; an actor with a belief about it carries a mood penalty.
  The contradiction is where the story is. *Farstone holds its contradictions, and that is the point.*
- **Emergent, not scripted.** The design philosophy is that the world dramatizes itself.
  Every system — the tick, needs, mood, utility-AI, resource depletion, the per-actor Umwelt, the bilattice fold — is built to turn initial conditions (scarcity, feud, contradiction) into stories without a hand on the wheel.
  Preserve this: seed conditions and dispositions, then let the world run.
- **Concrete and material.** Smoked fish, ship's biscuit, tarred rope, pig-iron, oak billets, a brass clasp-knife.
  Granite, fog, spartina, tern colonies, the freshwater plume past the breakwater.
  Specificity is the texture; keep it concrete.
- **Civic and procedural, without irony.** Charters, quorums, decision windows, reconciliations, mutual-aid agreements.
  Farstone takes its own small governance seriously, and the world should too — the drama of a permit deadline is played straight.

A line to keep in mind: the most interesting events on Farstone are *tides, fog, ferry schedules held up by weather, water-quality thresholds, permits aging past their decision window, and the slow work of keeping infrastructure intact.*

## Open Questions / Gaps

Recorded honestly where the archive is thin:

- **Deep family history.** The named-citizen sketch is believable but thin; no multi-generational settlement records or household trees recovered.
- **Incident history of the seeded conflicts.** What chain of events led to the Hydrant-23 dispute, the permit backlog, or the two dairies' chronic non-compliance is not documented.
- **Breakwater & ferry history.** When the breakwater was built, its full storm history, the ferry operator and schedule history, and ridership records are unrecorded.
- **Harbor ecology specifics.** Fish-population surveys, seasonal migration, market prices for catch, and historical fishing records are not quantified.
- **The granite quarry** (Tomas Strake's workplace in the MUD reading) has no recorded history or current operating status.
- **Theology of Maraset.** The grey-eyed lady of tide and harbor is named but her cult, doctrine, and festivals are not described.
- **Architecture.** The interior of the Victorian city hall, the oldest buildings in Farston, and the infrastructure beneath Main Street are undescribed.
- **Population trajectory.** Whether the island's population is stable, growing, or shrinking — and what drove historical settlement — is unrecorded.
- **Relationship between the readings.** How the current PyFARS/governance Farstone relates to the earlier tab-archive EAVT Farstone and the MUD/Tale variants — what was preserved and what was lost across migrations — is not settled.
