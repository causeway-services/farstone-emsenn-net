# Inhabitants of Farstone

This file is the durable record of who lives on Farstone Island and which factions bind them together.
It is **world data, not engine code**: names, dispositions, locations, relationships, and the dramatic tensions each person sits inside.
Any future engine should be able to seed a living world from this file alone.

## A note on NPCs as ludics designs

Every named non-player character here is meant to be read as a **ludics design / strategy** — not as a script of lines to recite, but as a stance toward the world: a set of dispositions (traits), a set of needs that decay over time, and a set of stakes it holds against other designs.
An NPC "plays" Farstone the way a strategy plays a game: it reacts to what it can perceive (its Umwelt), pursues its most pressing need, and presses its position against rivals.
Drama is meant to **emerge** from designs interacting under scarcity, contradiction, and feud — nobody writes the story, the interacting strategies produce it.
When you re-home these characters in a new engine, preserve the *stakes and dispositions*, not any particular dialogue.
The trait pairs (hot-headed vs. cold, ambitious vs. tenacious), the opposed material interests, and the contested facts are the load-bearing content.

Two readings of Farstone exist in the recovered material, and both are canon:

- The **governance reading** (PyFARS) — Farstone as a real municipality of ~12,000, with elected officials, appointed directors, a charter, and slow civic conflicts (water-quality disputes, permit deadlines, budget overruns).
- The **harbor MUD reading** — Farstone Harbor in the off-season, a ~30-room working waterfront whose cast is built from the constraints of lean-season maritime life.
  This reading itself has two variants: the hand-authored **Farstone-Harbor** world and the designed **Farstone-Tale** ("The Off-Season").

The same island holds both.
Where a character belongs to one reading, it is marked.
Where readings name the "same" role differently, both names are kept.

## Active Tensions & Seeded Arcs (Governance)

These are the designed dramatic engines of the governance reading.
Each is meant to exercise a specific mechanic; preserve them as live tensions, not resolved history.

1. **Hydrant-23 water-quality conflict** — Routine inspector passed it; contracted lab inspector failed it.
  The knowledge-meet of pass and fail produces **⊤ (TOP)** — a contradiction the world *holds* as a value.
  Director Chen has 24 hours to resolve via re-inspection; until resolved, the hydrant is treated as failing (precautionary principle).
2. **Riparian-buffer compliance (Farr Valley)** — Ben Jordan's dairy and one other (unnamed) cited for chronic non-compliance with the 30-m buffer ordinance.
  A slow-burn dispute that exercises anomaly-handling on a long timescale.
3. **Building-permit decision deadline (Ellis Pearce)** — One of Ellis's permits is past the 30-day charter window, a deliberate charter-binding-violation risk to test deadline-tracking.
  (Permit 2026-117, a rear-deck addition at 412 Cherry Lane, filed 2026-06-22, sits at 27 of 30 days elapsed.)
4. **Budget overrun (snow removal)** — Director Johnson flags it; Mayor Reyes tracks it; the FY2026 contingency line has $48k remaining.
5. **Storm-closure cascade** — The harbormaster's closure declaration triggers ferry suspension, mutual-aid activation, Harbor Cafe extended hours, library basement shelter readiness, and Tomás Vázquez appearing in multiple dispatch contexts at once.

## Farstone-Tale Cast — "The Off-Season" (designed world)

This variant foregrounds three pressures from tick zero — **scarce food** (the failing fishery), **a disputed permit** (dredging Application 23), and **a feud**.
The cast is shaped to dramatize those pressures without scripting.

  Half of the central feud.

- **Sister Edda — chapel healer.** Kind, patient.
  Tends the strained; gathers marsh-herbs.
  The relief valve on mood pressure.
- **Hale Branning — up-valley farmer.** Gruff, industrious.
  Holds the only surplus food on the island and resents the town.
  The second pole of the food-fight scarcity drama.
- **Old Fenwick — the failing fisher** (Tale recast of the lamp-keeper role).
  Anxious, stubborn; his nets come up empty; he digs for clams when the flat is exposed.
  The human face of scarcity.
- **The Drifter** — stranded for the season.
  Restless, sharp; can't leave till spring.
  Hungry (need 30), bored — a hungry wildcard on the lean-season stage.

## How the harbor cast is meant to play

Each NPC carries three needs (hunger fastest to decay, then rest, then recreation).
Mood = need satisfaction + trait modifiers + any contested-belief penalties.
A utility AI sends the hungriest design to the nearest food and depletes it; when a resource runs dry, the actors who lived off it go hungry, mood drops, and competition becomes real.
Rivals (Dax/Mara; Coilin/Brannaugh) hold opposed traits and shared contested ground, so feuds sharpen under scarcity on their own.
The disputed permit, once it ages past its window or is ruled against, settles to ⊤, and every actor holding a belief about it carries a contested-belief mood penalty.
The story is the interaction; the tick does the rest.
